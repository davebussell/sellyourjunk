Donating in Quebec

Where to donate used goods in Montréal

10 organizations in and around Montréal that take donated furniture, clothes, appliances, kitchenware and household goods. For each one: what they take, what they refuse, and whether they will come and collect it.

Two things worth knowing before you load the car. First, almost every large Montréal collector refuses furniture. Renaissance, Le Support and Entraide diabétique du Québec all say no to sofas, beds and mattresses. For large items the realistic options are Welcome Collective (paid collection, from $50, and the only one that will take a fridge or a stove), L'Entraide Les Ami(e)s de Montréal, Le Chaînon's furniture form, or a city écocentre. Second, the city's écocentres are the fallback for the awkward things the charities turn away: they accept goods in good condition for reuse — antiques, garden items, sports equipment, musical instruments (but not large ones like pianos or organs), toys and games, books, DVDs, records and CDs, furniture and decorative items, hand tools, small electrical appliances, clothing and household linen — and route them to the operator that runs Montréal's reuse centres. Find your nearest one at montreal.ca/sujets/ecocentres. Service is French-first at most of these organizations; Renaissance, Welcome Collective, Insertech and Le Support publish full English versions of their sites.

Verified 2026-09-24 from each organization's own website. Hours, fees, pickup areas and what is accepted change without notice — call or check their site before you load the car. We are not affiliated with any organization listed here and we receive nothing if you donate to them.

If you cannot move it yourself

These 6 will come to you in Montréal. Everyone else on this page is drop-off.

  • Entraide diabétique du Québec — Free home collection, in their own words: "Nous collectons vos vêtements et articles usagés tout à fait gratuitement" and "Nul besoin de vous déplacer! Nous venons à vous gratuitement." You register through the form on their site, they call you back to agree a date, and on the day you leave the bags at the agreed spot marked with a paper bearing the letter "D" for "Diabète." No time is given in advance — the driver sets it that day. The Mirabel office covers part of Montréal, Laval, the Rive-Nord, part of the Montérégie and the Laurentides: 450 530-7800 or 1 866 313-7800.
  • Insertech — Free pickup is for businesses only: partner companies, or donations of at least 15 computers with an Intel i5 8th-generation processor or better. Everyone else drops off at the back door, Monday to Friday 8 a.m. to 4 p.m., closed Wednesdays, no appointment needed.
  • L'Entraide Les Ami(e)s de Montréal — They collect furniture and accessories in good condition from homes across Greater Montréal, Laval and the South Shore: "Nous ramassons seulement des meubles et accessoires en bon état." No fee is stated on their site and they do not publish a list of what they turn away, so confirm both when you call. (514) 846-9222 or (514) 507-3200, Monday to Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., closed weekends.
  • Le Chaînon — Furniture is handled separately: you fill in the furniture donation form on their site and, in their words, "a responsible person will communicate with you" to arrange the details. No terms or fee are published, so ask when they call. General donations go to the stores during opening hours; phone 514-843-4354 to arrange a large drop-off.
  • Le Support (Société québécoise de la déficience intellectuelle) — They do run a home pickup service — their site describes arranging a time and coming to your home to collect what you have to give, booked through an online application. No fee is stated on the pages that loaded, and the booking page itself did not load when checked, so confirm terms and any cost when you book. Call 514 725-9797, Monday to Friday 8 a.m. to 3:15 p.m., to check whether a specific item is accepted.
  • Welcome Collective — One of the few Montréal charities that will come for a sofa or a fridge. Their own wording: "Our collection rates begin at $50, and include a sliding scale option." They cover the island of Montréal, the South Shore and Laval; pickups run Monday to Friday, 9:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. Drop-off is also possible at their Ahuntsic warehouse near métro Sauvé, Monday to Friday 10 a.m. to 3 p.m.

Before you load the car

  • Phone first if it is bulky. Capacity changes week to week, and a sofa that cannot be accepted is a wasted trip for you and a wasted hour for them.
  • Clean it. Most refusals are about condition, not category. Textiles must be dry and odour-free or they get thrown out at the back door.
  • Take the parts. Bed frames need their hardware, and a dresser without its drawers is scrap to almost everyone.
  • Do not leave it outside after hours. Anything left at a closed door usually gets rained on, and the charity pays to dispose of it.
  • Ask about the receipt before you hand it over, not after.

Not sure it should be donated at all?

Some of what people give away is worth real money, and some of it will cost a charity money to dispose of. Photograph it and we will tell you which — including when the honest answer is that it is worth nothing and a thrift store is exactly the right place for it. We earn nothing when you donate.

Common questions

Will any of them pick it up from my house in Montréal?
6 of the 10 organizations on this page collect from your home: Entraide diabétique du Québec, Insertech, L'Entraide Les Ami(e)s de Montréal, Le Chaînon, Le Support (Société québécoise de la déficience intellectuelle), Welcome Collective. Collection usually depends on what the item is, what condition it is in, and whether you are inside their catchment. Phone first.
Do I get a tax receipt for donated goods?
Registered charities can issue a receipt for the fair market value of a gift in kind, but many thrift operations do not issue receipts for ordinary household donations because valuing them individually is not practical. Ask before you drop off if the receipt matters to you, and do not assume.
What will nobody take?
Stained or torn upholstery, mattresses in most of the country, broken particleboard furniture, exercise equipment, cribs and car seats past their expiry, and anything that has been in a fire or a flood. Most organizations list their refusals on their own site, and the list above repeats them where they do.
Is my stuff worth money instead of donating it?
Sometimes, and it is worth thirty seconds to find out. A dead car, a garage of tools or mid-century teak can be worth real money. A sofa is usually worth nothing, no matter how good it looks. We will tell you which one you have got before you decide.
