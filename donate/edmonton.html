Donating in Alberta

Where to donate used goods in Edmonton

11 organizations in and around Edmonton that take donated furniture, clothes, appliances, kitchenware and household goods. For each one: what they take, what they refuse, and whether they will come and collect it.

Edmonton has exactly one confirmed free home-collection service for bulky household goods: Habitat for Humanity Edmonton's ReStore, which runs its own trucks across Edmonton, Beaumont, Fort Saskatchewan, Leduc (twice monthly), Nisku, St. Albert, Sherwood Park and Spruce Grove. Their own site states the crucial limit: "Due to safety regulations, we unfortunately cannot move any items from basements, nor can we enter homes." The sofa has to be at ground level or in the garage before they arrive. Beyond that, most Edmonton charities are drop-off only. Goodwill states plainly on its own site that it has no pickup service at all, despite operating 14 donation centres in the city. Find Edmonton will collect on weekends in surrounding areas but reserves the right to refuse items at the door, and its own page warns that items that don't qualify may be offered "disposal for a fee" - so cost is not guaranteed to be zero. Diabetes Canada's free pickup is a clothing-and-small-goods service only, on strict terms: sealed bags or boxes under 40 lbs on the doorstep, and "Loose items will not be collected."

Verified 2026-09-24 from each organization's own website. Hours, fees, pickup areas and what is accepted change without notice — call or check their site before you load the car. We are not affiliated with any organization listed here and we receive nothing if you donate to them.

If you cannot move it yourself

These 6 will come to you in Edmonton. Everyone else on this page is drop-off.

  • Diabetes Canada - Declutter for Diabetes — Free home collection, but only when they have a scheduled run in your area — enter your postal code on their site to check. Items must be in sealed bags or boxes under 40 lb and left on the doorstep; loose items are not collected.
  • Edmonton Emergency Response and Newcomer Services (EERNS) — They arrange furniture pickups from your location. Email donations@eerns.ca to arrange. Their site does not state whether a fee applies or which areas are covered, so confirm both when you book.
  • Edmonton's Food Bank — Their site states: "To maximize our trucking resources, we can not arrange pick ups for small amounts of food (i.e. less than 100 lbs)." For donations over 100 lbs, contact them with at least 3 business days' notice so they can route a truck. Household-sized donations must be dropped off.
  • Electronic Recycling Association (ERA) — Pickups are offered to both residents and businesses, Monday to Friday, and are aimed at larger quantities - their site says ERA "will dispatch a truck to pickup large quantities of electronics". Charges apply: "Any pickup charges applied to your donations are based on location, the number of items, service requirements." Drop-offs of standard computer equipment are often free. Budget for a fee unless the donation is high-value or high-volume. Working devices are refurbished and may be donated on to charities, schools and non-profits; secure data destruction is offered, priced from $0.00 to $5.00 per item depending on the equipment.
  • Find Edmonton — Their own donation form page states: "We are happy to pick up of in the surrounding areas of Edmonton on weekends." Book at https://findedmonton.com/pages/donation-form or call 780-988-1717. Important caveat from the same page: "The Find trucking team have discretion not to accept an item if it does not meet our donation criteria" - and donors whose items do not qualify may be offered other options "for a fee". Whether the pickup itself is free is not stated, so confirm when you book.
  • Habitat for Humanity Edmonton ReStore — Free donation pickup covering Edmonton, Beaumont, Fort Saskatchewan, Leduc (twice monthly), Nisku, St. Albert, Sherwood Park and Spruce Grove. Critical limitation stated on their own site: "Due to safety regulations, we unfortunately cannot move any items from basements, nor can we enter homes." Items must be accessible outside the home. Book through their online form or call (780) 477-4057.

Before you load the car

  • Phone first if it is bulky. Capacity changes week to week, and a sofa that cannot be accepted is a wasted trip for you and a wasted hour for them.
  • Clean it. Most refusals are about condition, not category. Textiles must be dry and odour-free or they get thrown out at the back door.
  • Take the parts. Bed frames need their hardware, and a dresser without its drawers is scrap to almost everyone.
  • Do not leave it outside after hours. Anything left at a closed door usually gets rained on, and the charity pays to dispose of it.
  • Ask about the receipt before you hand it over, not after.

Not sure it should be donated at all?

Some of what people give away is worth real money, and some of it will cost a charity money to dispose of. Photograph it and we will tell you which — including when the honest answer is that it is worth nothing and a thrift store is exactly the right place for it. We earn nothing when you donate.

Common questions

Will any of them pick it up from my house in Edmonton?
6 of the 11 organizations on this page collect from your home: Diabetes Canada - Declutter for Diabetes, Edmonton Emergency Response and Newcomer Services (EERNS), Edmonton's Food Bank, Electronic Recycling Association (ERA), Find Edmonton, Habitat for Humanity Edmonton ReStore. Collection usually depends on what the item is, what condition it is in, and whether you are inside their catchment. Phone first.
Do I get a tax receipt for donated goods?
Registered charities can issue a receipt for the fair market value of a gift in kind, but many thrift operations do not issue receipts for ordinary household donations because valuing them individually is not practical. Ask before you drop off if the receipt matters to you, and do not assume.
What will nobody take?
Stained or torn upholstery, mattresses in most of the country, broken particleboard furniture, exercise equipment, cribs and car seats past their expiry, and anything that has been in a fire or a flood. Most organizations list their refusals on their own site, and the list above repeats them where they do.
Is my stuff worth money instead of donating it?
Sometimes, and it is worth thirty seconds to find out. A dead car, a garage of tools or mid-century teak can be worth real money. A sofa is usually worth nothing, no matter how good it looks. We will tell you which one you have got before you decide.
