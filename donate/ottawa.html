Donating in Ontario

Where to donate used goods in Ottawa

14 organizations in and around Ottawa that take donated furniture, clothes, appliances, kitchenware and household goods. For each one: what they take, what they refuse, and whether they will come and collect it.

Ottawa's hard case is a sofa, and the reason is that the two biggest names take neither. The Salvation Army lists furniture on its cannot-accept list outright and states it is unable to offer any pickup at all; Diabetes Canada's Declutter will come to your door, but refuses all furniture and all beds. That leaves three realistic routes for large furniture, each with a different catch: Habitat ReStore (free pickup, but selective, one to two weeks' notice, and their form currently restricts pickup to kitchens and large appliances), Helping With Furniture (drop-off free, pickup carries a service fee and only inside their zone), and Matthew House Ottawa (drop-off by arrangement, pickup number published but no price or zones published anywhere). Across the river, Comptoir Saint-Vincent de Paul de Hull is the standout: it takes furniture, mattresses, fridges and stoves together, and its trucks collect in the Hull, Aylmer and Gatineau sectors. Its site is French-only. Two smaller routes are worth knowing because they save a trip: any Ottawa Public Library branch takes small book donations for FOPLA, and any Browns Cleaners location takes used children's snowsuits, launders them and delivers them to The Snowsuit Fund for you.

Verified 2026-09-24 from each organization's own website. Hours, fees, pickup areas and what is accepted change without notice — call or check their site before you load the car. We are not affiliated with any organization listed here and we receive nothing if you donate to them.

If you cannot move it yourself

These 7 will come to you in Ottawa. Everyone else on this page is drop-off.

  • Comptoir Saint-Vincent de Paul de Hull — The main option on the Quebec side, and one of the few places anywhere in the region that takes furniture, mattresses and large appliances together. Their trucks collect donated items in good condition in the Hull, Aylmer and Gatineau sectors — phone 819-776-1068 to arrange. No fee is stated for donation pickup; delivery of items you buy is offered at reasonable cost. Site is in French.
  • Diabetes Canada — Declutter (Diabetes Clothesline) — Free home collection, but only when they have a scheduled run in your area — enter your postal code on their site to check. Items must be in sealed bags or boxes under 40 lb and left on the doorstep; loose items are not collected.
  • Electronic Recycling Association (ERA) — Ottawa — Pickup exists but is not automatically free: ERA will dispatch a truck for large quantities of electronics, and charges are based on location, the number of items and the services required. They note costs are sometimes waived during their yearly promotions or for members and high-value donations. Book through the pickup form on their page or call 613-518-4467 (toll-free 1-877-9EWASTE). What distinguishes them from a shredder is that working devices are wiped and refurbished for donation rather than destroyed.
  • Habitat for Humanity Greater Ottawa ReStore — Pickup is offered free of charge, but their own form page says not all donations qualify and — read this before you plan around it — that pickup is CURRENTLY LIMITED TO KITCHENS AND LARGE APPLIANCES under restrictions the page still attributes to COVID-19. That notice may be stale, so call 613-749-9950 or email donation@habitatgo.com before assuming a sofa will be collected. Terms they do state: requests are answered in 24-48 hours, pickups generally need one to two weeks' notice, items must be ground level and accessible or have service elevator access, must be free of rips, stains, odours and pet hair, and the driver has final approval. Request form at https://habitatgo.com/restore/restore-pick-up-form/. They also advise calling before donating, since what they can take shifts with stock levels.
  • Helping With Furniture / Aidons avec des meubles — A service fee applies. Their booking page states plainly that a service fee will apply and that your address must fall inside the HWF designated pickup zone; you enter the address in the map search at https://www.hwfottawa.org/eng/request-a-pickup and, if it is in the zone, complete the request form. For addresses outside the zone they refer donors to an independent mover. Free drop-off during the two weekly windows is the cheaper route. The fee amount is not published — confirm when you book.
  • Shepherds of Good Hope — They will collect, but only in defined circumstances: if you are elderly, disabled or without transportation, or if the donation is too large to transport safely. Their stated limit is a 30-minute driving radius of the downtown location. Arrange it by emailing donate@sghottawa.com. Drop-offs are taken between 8:00 a.m. and 4:00 p.m.
  • The Ottawa Mission — They can arrange donation pick-ups within Ottawa on request — call 613-234-1144 ext. 428 or email donations@ottawamission.com. Worth knowing before you load the car: this is a men's shelter with limited storage, and household goods, furniture and used clothing are all on the cannot-accept list. New items are what they are asking for.

Before you load the car

  • Phone first if it is bulky. Capacity changes week to week, and a sofa that cannot be accepted is a wasted trip for you and a wasted hour for them.
  • Clean it. Most refusals are about condition, not category. Textiles must be dry and odour-free or they get thrown out at the back door.
  • Take the parts. Bed frames need their hardware, and a dresser without its drawers is scrap to almost everyone.
  • Do not leave it outside after hours. Anything left at a closed door usually gets rained on, and the charity pays to dispose of it.
  • Ask about the receipt before you hand it over, not after.

Not sure it should be donated at all?

Some of what people give away is worth real money, and some of it will cost a charity money to dispose of. Photograph it and we will tell you which — including when the honest answer is that it is worth nothing and a thrift store is exactly the right place for it. We earn nothing when you donate.

Common questions

Will any of them pick it up from my house in Ottawa?
7 of the 14 organizations on this page collect from your home: Comptoir Saint-Vincent de Paul de Hull, Diabetes Canada — Declutter (Diabetes Clothesline), Electronic Recycling Association (ERA) — Ottawa, Habitat for Humanity Greater Ottawa ReStore, Helping With Furniture / Aidons avec des meubles, Shepherds of Good Hope, The Ottawa Mission. Collection usually depends on what the item is, what condition it is in, and whether you are inside their catchment. Phone first.
Do I get a tax receipt for donated goods?
Registered charities can issue a receipt for the fair market value of a gift in kind, but many thrift operations do not issue receipts for ordinary household donations because valuing them individually is not practical. Ask before you drop off if the receipt matters to you, and do not assume.
What will nobody take?
Stained or torn upholstery, mattresses in most of the country, broken particleboard furniture, exercise equipment, cribs and car seats past their expiry, and anything that has been in a fire or a flood. Most organizations list their refusals on their own site, and the list above repeats them where they do.
Is my stuff worth money instead of donating it?
Sometimes, and it is worth thirty seconds to find out. A dead car, a garage of tools or mid-century teak can be worth real money. A sofa is usually worth nothing, no matter how good it looks. We will tell you which one you have got before you decide.
