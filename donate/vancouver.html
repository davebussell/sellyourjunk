Donating in British Columbia

Where to donate used goods in Vancouver

13 organizations in and around Vancouver that take donated furniture, clothes, appliances, kitchenware and household goods. For each one: what they take, what they refuse, and whether they will come and collect it.

Furniture is the real Vancouver problem, and it is worth saying so at the top of the page. Salvation Army, MCC Surrey and the Surrey Community Thrift Store all refuse furniture outright; BC SPCA takes "small furniture items" only and refuses mattresses. A sofa realistically means Habitat ReStore's free pickup, or paying a furniture bank to come and get it - HomeStart (minimum $100; Vancouver, Burnaby west of Royal Oak, and Richmond) or Shelter to Home (minimum $130; North and West Vancouver only, but they do take mattresses, which almost nobody else does). The only genuinely free at-home collections verified are Big Brothers of Greater Vancouver and Diabetes Canada, and both are limited to clothing, books and small household goods - neither will take a couch. Goodwill does not operate in BC; do not list it.

Verified 2026-09-24 from each organization's own website. Hours, fees, pickup areas and what is accepted change without notice — call or check their site before you load the car. We are not affiliated with any organization listed here and we receive nothing if you donate to them.

If you cannot move it yourself

These 6 will come to you in Vancouver. Everyone else on this page is drop-off.

  • Big Brothers of Greater Vancouver — Their page states: "Free service available for clothing, books, and small household goods. Book a pickup online or call 604.526.2447." Note the pickup is limited to clothing, books and small household goods - not furniture. Bagging/labelling instructions and a pickup time were not confirmed on the page read.
  • Diabetes Canada - Declutter clothing donation programme — Free home collection, but only when they have a scheduled run in your area — enter your postal code on their site to check. Items must be in sealed bags or boxes under 40 lb and left on the doorstep; loose items are not collected.
  • Electronic Recycling Association (ERA) — Pickup is offered but is NOT automatically free. Their page: pickup charges are "based on location, the number of items, service requirements, etc.", and may be waived during promotions or for members and high-value donations. Dropping standard computer equipment at the Richmond depot is typically free, but processing fees apply to TVs, microwaves and floor-model copiers and printers. Data destruction is quoted at $0.00-$5.00 per item depending on the equipment donated. Get a quote before loading a vehicle.
  • HomeStart Foundation — Pickup for a minimum $100 donation. Drivers accept cash, cheque or credit card, or you can donate online in advance. Service area as written on their page: "Vancouver, Burnaby (west of Royal Oak) and Richmond". Book by emailing coordinator@homestart.ca to arrange pickup days and times. Note: pickup days/hours and a same-day policy were NOT found on the page read - do not publish specific hours.
  • Shelter to Home — Their page states: "Once approved, we can pick up from your location for a minimum $130 donation (to cover moving costs)" and "we are able to pick up furniture only from North and West Vancouver locations." Email Info@sheltertohome.ca or phone 778-653-9669 with photos and dimensions to get approval first.
  • Union Gospel Mission (UGM) — Their page states: "Due to capacity, we are not able to fulfill every request for donation pick-ups." Requests go through the donation coordination form on that page. A pickup is not guaranteed and no terms, area or fee are published.

Before you load the car

  • Phone first if it is bulky. Capacity changes week to week, and a sofa that cannot be accepted is a wasted trip for you and a wasted hour for them.
  • Clean it. Most refusals are about condition, not category. Textiles must be dry and odour-free or they get thrown out at the back door.
  • Take the parts. Bed frames need their hardware, and a dresser without its drawers is scrap to almost everyone.
  • Do not leave it outside after hours. Anything left at a closed door usually gets rained on, and the charity pays to dispose of it.
  • Ask about the receipt before you hand it over, not after.

Not sure it should be donated at all?

Some of what people give away is worth real money, and some of it will cost a charity money to dispose of. Photograph it and we will tell you which — including when the honest answer is that it is worth nothing and a thrift store is exactly the right place for it. We earn nothing when you donate.

Common questions

Will any of them pick it up from my house in Vancouver?
6 of the 13 organizations on this page collect from your home: Big Brothers of Greater Vancouver, Diabetes Canada - Declutter clothing donation programme, Electronic Recycling Association (ERA), HomeStart Foundation, Shelter to Home, Union Gospel Mission (UGM). Collection usually depends on what the item is, what condition it is in, and whether you are inside their catchment. Phone first.
Do I get a tax receipt for donated goods?
Registered charities can issue a receipt for the fair market value of a gift in kind, but many thrift operations do not issue receipts for ordinary household donations because valuing them individually is not practical. Ask before you drop off if the receipt matters to you, and do not assume.
What will nobody take?
Stained or torn upholstery, mattresses in most of the country, broken particleboard furniture, exercise equipment, cribs and car seats past their expiry, and anything that has been in a fire or a flood. Most organizations list their refusals on their own site, and the list above repeats them where they do.
Is my stuff worth money instead of donating it?
Sometimes, and it is worth thirty seconds to find out. A dead car, a garage of tools or mid-century teak can be worth real money. A sofa is usually worth nothing, no matter how good it looks. We will tell you which one you have got before you decide.
