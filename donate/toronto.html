Donating in Ontario

Where to donate used goods in Toronto

13 organizations in and around Toronto that take donated furniture, clothes, appliances, kitchenware and household goods. For each one: what they take, what they refuse, and whether they will come and collect it.

The most useful thing to tell a Toronto reader is which routes actually get a large item out of the house, because the popular assumption is wrong. The Salvation Army — the charity most Torontonians still picture sending a truck — states outright on its own site that it cannot offer pick-up services and does not accept furniture at all. Talize, Value Village and Dress for Success also confirm no home pickup. In the GTA the verified ways to move furniture are: Habitat ReStore (free curbside, or paid in-home from $150), Furniture Bank (paid full-service in-home from $150, and the fee funds the charity), Royal Furniture Bank (genuinely free pickup, but Peel Region only — Brampton, Mississauga, Caledon), and Diabetes Canada (free pickup, but clothing and small household items only, in sealed bags under 40 lbs). Second point worth making: Value Village is a for-profit retailer that pays a nonprofit partner for your goods, and the partner differs store to store — at the Queen St E centre it is the Kidney Foundation of Canada. That is not a reason to avoid it, but readers assuming they are donating to a charity should know.

Verified 2026-09-24 from each organization's own website. Hours, fees, pickup areas and what is accepted change without notice — call or check their site before you load the car. We are not affiliated with any organization listed here and we receive nothing if you donate to them.

If you cannot move it yourself

These 7 will come to you in Toronto. Everyone else on this page is drop-off.

  • Diabetes Canada — Declutter for Diabetes — Free home collection, but only when they have a scheduled run in your area — enter your postal code on their site to check. Items must be in sealed bags or boxes under 40 lb and left on the doorstep; loose items are not collected.
  • Electronic Recycling Association (ERA) — Pickup is offered for larger quantities but is NOT free — charges depend on location, number of items and services required. They say costs are sometimes waived during promotions. Call before assuming a free collection.
  • Furniture Bank — Full-service in-home pickup, charged as a volume-based fee starting at $150. The fee funds the charity. A free drop-off option at their warehouse also exists, by scheduled appointment.
  • Habitat for Humanity GTA ReStore — Curbside pickup is free. In-home pickup is a paid service, priced on their donate page as $150 for 3 items or less, $300 for 4-7 items, and $450 for more than 8. Book through the online donation pick-up request form.
  • New Circles Community Services (GLOW clothing bank) — "Pick-up service is not typically available, except by prior arrangement for large drives." Otherwise drop off at the loading dock. No appointment needed within the drop-off hours.
  • Royal Furniture Bank — Free pickup from your home, booked through their online form. Verified on their own schedule-pickup page: "We'll come to you. Schedule a free pickup from your home." Note their homepage separately describes a $300 delivery fee — that fee is charged to the FAMILIES RECEIVING furniture, not to donors. Do not present the $300 as a donor cost.
  • reBOOT Canada — Pickup is for organizations donating 20 or more items only, and fees may apply. Individuals drop off. Back up your data first — devices are sanitized.

Before you load the car

  • Phone first if it is bulky. Capacity changes week to week, and a sofa that cannot be accepted is a wasted trip for you and a wasted hour for them.
  • Clean it. Most refusals are about condition, not category. Textiles must be dry and odour-free or they get thrown out at the back door.
  • Take the parts. Bed frames need their hardware, and a dresser without its drawers is scrap to almost everyone.
  • Do not leave it outside after hours. Anything left at a closed door usually gets rained on, and the charity pays to dispose of it.
  • Ask about the receipt before you hand it over, not after.

Not sure it should be donated at all?

Some of what people give away is worth real money, and some of it will cost a charity money to dispose of. Photograph it and we will tell you which — including when the honest answer is that it is worth nothing and a thrift store is exactly the right place for it. We earn nothing when you donate.

Common questions

Will any of them pick it up from my house in Toronto?
7 of the 13 organizations on this page collect from your home: Diabetes Canada — Declutter for Diabetes, Electronic Recycling Association (ERA), Furniture Bank, Habitat for Humanity GTA ReStore, New Circles Community Services (GLOW clothing bank), Royal Furniture Bank, reBOOT Canada. Collection usually depends on what the item is, what condition it is in, and whether you are inside their catchment. Phone first.
Do I get a tax receipt for donated goods?
Registered charities can issue a receipt for the fair market value of a gift in kind, but many thrift operations do not issue receipts for ordinary household donations because valuing them individually is not practical. Ask before you drop off if the receipt matters to you, and do not assume.
What will nobody take?
Stained or torn upholstery, mattresses in most of the country, broken particleboard furniture, exercise equipment, cribs and car seats past their expiry, and anything that has been in a fire or a flood. Most organizations list their refusals on their own site, and the list above repeats them where they do.
Is my stuff worth money instead of donating it?
Sometimes, and it is worth thirty seconds to find out. A dead car, a garage of tools or mid-century teak can be worth real money. A sofa is usually worth nothing, no matter how good it looks. We will tell you which one you have got before you decide.
