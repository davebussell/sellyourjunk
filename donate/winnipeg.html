Donating in Manitoba

Where to donate used goods in Winnipeg

13 organizations in and around Winnipeg that take donated furniture, clothes, appliances, kitchenware and household goods. For each one: what they take, what they refuse, and whether they will come and collect it.

Winnipeg's biggest quirk is that Goodwill here is not the Goodwill people know from elsewhere - Canadian Goodwill Industries is a registered Manitoba non-profit, Canadian since 1931, with six Manitoba stores, all proceeds staying in the province, and genuinely free home pickup that takes furniture, mattresses and large freestanding appliances. Between it and Habitat ReStore, which offers free pickup within city limits, Winnipeggers have two no-cost options for heavy items, with Oyate Tipi as a paid-but-receipted third that will actually carry a sofa out of the house. The trap worth knowing: Salvation Army Thrift and Value Village take no furniture and do no pickups at all - Salvation Army says so in plain words on its own donation page. One genuinely local arrangement no national list would have: bikes and bike parts can be left at any City of Winnipeg 4R Depot, where the WRENCH collects them about twice a month.

Verified 2026-09-24 from each organization's own website. Hours, fees, pickup areas and what is accepted change without notice — call or check their site before you load the car. We are not affiliated with any organization listed here and we receive nothing if you donate to them.

If you cannot move it yourself

These 5 will come to you in Winnipeg. Everyone else on this page is drop-off.

  • Canadian Goodwill Industries — Free home pickup, booked by phone at (204) 943-6435, by email, or through their online request form, Monday to Friday 8am-4pm. Pickup runs on a city district schedule that sets which day your area is served, and you get a confirmation email with your date within 48 business hours. Service covers Winnipeg and select nearby Manitoba communities. All donations are inspected for rodent and insect activity - they will not collect from a home that has had pest activity or extermination in the last 3 months. Read their pickup terms and conditions before booking.
  • Diabetes Canada - Declutter for Diabetes — Free home collection, but only when they have a scheduled run in your area — enter your postal code on their site to check. Items must be in sealed bags or boxes under 40 lb and left on the doorstep; loose items are not collected.
  • Electronic Recycling Association (ERA) — They do send trucks across Winnipeg to collect electronics from offices, warehouses, schools and residential addresses, booked online or by phone - but pickup is not automatically free. Cost depends on your location, the number of items and the level of service required, and fees are frequently waived during promotions and for members or high-value donations. For a single old laptop, drop it off instead.
  • Habitat for Humanity Manitoba ReStore — Free pickup of donations and larger items within city limits, including curbside. Items must be readily accessible - outside or in a garage; crews do not retrieve items from basements or other levels. A ReStore driver inspects each item and makes the final decision on what is taken. Book by calling Sheila at (204) 233-5160 ext. 220.
  • Oyate Tipi Cumini Yape — They will come and carry it out, but pickup is a paid service - they describe their rates as competitive, 100% of the fee goes to their charity operations, you pay no HST, and you receive a charitable tax receipt for the value of the furniture. Request a pickup through their site or call. For a sofa you cannot move yourself, this is usually the realistic Winnipeg option.

Before you load the car

  • Phone first if it is bulky. Capacity changes week to week, and a sofa that cannot be accepted is a wasted trip for you and a wasted hour for them.
  • Clean it. Most refusals are about condition, not category. Textiles must be dry and odour-free or they get thrown out at the back door.
  • Take the parts. Bed frames need their hardware, and a dresser without its drawers is scrap to almost everyone.
  • Do not leave it outside after hours. Anything left at a closed door usually gets rained on, and the charity pays to dispose of it.
  • Ask about the receipt before you hand it over, not after.

Not sure it should be donated at all?

Some of what people give away is worth real money, and some of it will cost a charity money to dispose of. Photograph it and we will tell you which — including when the honest answer is that it is worth nothing and a thrift store is exactly the right place for it. We earn nothing when you donate.

Common questions

Will any of them pick it up from my house in Winnipeg?
5 of the 13 organizations on this page collect from your home: Canadian Goodwill Industries, Diabetes Canada - Declutter for Diabetes, Electronic Recycling Association (ERA), Habitat for Humanity Manitoba ReStore, Oyate Tipi Cumini Yape. Collection usually depends on what the item is, what condition it is in, and whether you are inside their catchment. Phone first.
Do I get a tax receipt for donated goods?
Registered charities can issue a receipt for the fair market value of a gift in kind, but many thrift operations do not issue receipts for ordinary household donations because valuing them individually is not practical. Ask before you drop off if the receipt matters to you, and do not assume.
What will nobody take?
Stained or torn upholstery, mattresses in most of the country, broken particleboard furniture, exercise equipment, cribs and car seats past their expiry, and anything that has been in a fire or a flood. Most organizations list their refusals on their own site, and the list above repeats them where they do.
Is my stuff worth money instead of donating it?
Sometimes, and it is worth thirty seconds to find out. A dead car, a garage of tools or mid-century teak can be worth real money. A sofa is usually worth nothing, no matter how good it looks. We will tell you which one you have got before you decide.
