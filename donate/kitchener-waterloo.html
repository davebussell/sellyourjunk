Donating in Ontario

Where to donate used goods in Kitchener–Waterloo

13 organizations in and around Kitchener–Waterloo that take donated furniture, clothes, appliances, kitchenware and household goods. For each one: what they take, what they refuse, and whether they will come and collect it.

The most useful local fact is MCC Picks Up (mccpicksup.com, 226-220-0088), a home pickup service run by Mennonite Central Committee that books online and covers, in their words, "the Region of Waterloo and some outlier communities." Two hard limits: it will NOT take furniture, and nothing may exceed 50 lb per container. Their FAQ does not state a price, so ask when you book. For furniture that genuinely needs collecting, the free option is St Vincent de Paul in Cambridge (one week's notice, main-floor access only, pickups Monday to Friday 9 a.m.-2 p.m.); Habitat for Humanity Guelph Wellington advertises free pickup outright; Habitat Waterloo Region takes pickup requests but warns that not all will qualify; and Mission Thrift Store Kitchener charges a flat $30 and collects from your curb. One trap catches people here: the Salvation Army thrift chain in Canada accepts neither furniture nor mattresses and states plainly that it offers no pickup at all, despite being most people's first guess. Mattresses are refused nearly everywhere - Worth A Second Look, run by The Working Centre at 37 Market Lane in downtown Kitchener, is the notable exception that lists mattresses and beds among what it accepts.

Verified 2026-09-24 from each organization's own website. Hours, fees, pickup areas and what is accepted change without notice — call or check their site before you load the car. We are not affiliated with any organization listed here and we receive nothing if you donate to them.

If you cannot move it yourself

These 10 will come to you in Kitchener–Waterloo. Everyone else on this page is drop-off.

  • Computer Recycling (The Working Centre) — "For large amounts of usable items we may be able to come pick it up. However, most items we ask you to drop off at our Computer Recycling location." Contact cr@theworkingcentre.org first.
  • Diabetes Canada (Declutter for Diabetes) — Free home collection, but only when they have a scheduled run in your area — enter your postal code on their site to check. Items must be in sealed bags or boxes under 40 lb and left on the doorstep; loose items are not collected.

Donations must be easily accessible and outside the home - "utilizing porches, driveways, lobbies and garages whenever possible." They request an optional monetary donation on completion to help cover costs.

No fee is stated anywhere on their donations or salvage pages - a testimonial on the salvage page describes a removal as free of charge. Confirm cost when you book.
  • MCC Picks Up — Home pickup of household goods, booked online. Their FAQ states they are "currently operating MCC Picks Up within the Region of Waterloo and some outlier communities" and invites suggestions for expansion by email. The FAQ does NOT state whether the pickup is free - confirm when booking. Nothing may exceed 50 lb per container, and furniture is excluded.

Book by calling (519) 579-4116 ext. 2 or emailing kitchener_furniture@missionthriftstore.com. Pickup window is Monday to Saturday 12:00pm-2:00pm, closed Sunday.
  • St Vincent de Paul Thrift Store, Cambridge — "Yes! We are happy to pick up donations of large items such as furniture-and for FREE! We require at least 1 week notice." Book via their furniture pickup request form, by email, in store, or by phoning 519-629-0103. Pickups are made Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 2 p.m. "All items must be easily accessible from a main floor entrance. We do not carry anything that requires going up or down flights of stairs inside a house. A few steps are ok."
  • The Food Bank of Waterloo Region — Not a general household pickup service. For frozen food donations they ask you to "Call 519.743.5576 or email info@thefoodbank.ca to discuss pickup options and make arrangements."
  • Worth A Second Look Furniture and Housewares (The Working Centre) — "If you have a large number of items and are not able to bring them to the store, please send us a photo and description of the items and we may be able to come pick them up." No fee is stated. Email wasl@theworkingcentre.org or phone the store.

Before you load the car

  • Phone first if it is bulky. Capacity changes week to week, and a sofa that cannot be accepted is a wasted trip for you and a wasted hour for them.
  • Clean it. Most refusals are about condition, not category. Textiles must be dry and odour-free or they get thrown out at the back door.
  • Take the parts. Bed frames need their hardware, and a dresser without its drawers is scrap to almost everyone.
  • Do not leave it outside after hours. Anything left at a closed door usually gets rained on, and the charity pays to dispose of it.
  • Ask about the receipt before you hand it over, not after.

Not sure it should be donated at all?

Some of what people give away is worth real money, and some of it will cost a charity money to dispose of. Photograph it and we will tell you which — including when the honest answer is that it is worth nothing and a thrift store is exactly the right place for it. We earn nothing when you donate.

Common questions

Will any of them pick it up from my house in Kitchener–Waterloo?
10 of the 13 organizations on this page collect from your home: Computer Recycling (The Working Centre), Diabetes Canada (Declutter for Diabetes), Habitat for Humanity Guelph Wellington ReStore, Habitat for Humanity Waterloo Region ReStore, MCC Picks Up, MCC Thrift on Kent, Mission Thrift Store Kitchener, St Vincent de Paul Thrift Store, Cambridge, The Food Bank of Waterloo Region, Worth A Second Look Furniture and Housewares (The Working Centre). Collection usually depends on what the item is, what condition it is in, and whether you are inside their catchment. Phone first.
Do I get a tax receipt for donated goods?
Registered charities can issue a receipt for the fair market value of a gift in kind, but many thrift operations do not issue receipts for ordinary household donations because valuing them individually is not practical. Ask before you drop off if the receipt matters to you, and do not assume.
What will nobody take?
Stained or torn upholstery, mattresses in most of the country, broken particleboard furniture, exercise equipment, cribs and car seats past their expiry, and anything that has been in a fire or a flood. Most organizations list their refusals on their own site, and the list above repeats them where they do.
Is my stuff worth money instead of donating it?
Sometimes, and it is worth thirty seconds to find out. A dead car, a garage of tools or mid-century teak can be worth real money. A sofa is usually worth nothing, no matter how good it looks. We will tell you which one you have got before you decide.
