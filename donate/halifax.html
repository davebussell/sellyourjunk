Where to donate used goods in Halifax

11 organizations in and around Halifax that take donated furniture, clothes, appliances, kitchenware and household goods. For each one: what they take, what they refuse, and whether they will come and collect it.

Verified 2026-09-24 from each organization's own website. Hours, fees, pickup areas and what is accepted change without notice — call or check their site before you load the car. We are not affiliated with any organization listed here and we receive nothing if you donate to them.

If you cannot move it yourself

These 4 will come to you in Halifax. Everyone else on this page is drop-off.

  • Habitat for Humanity Nova Scotia ReStore — Verified on their donation page: "drop off your items at our store during business hours or schedule a free donation pickup for larger items." To book, fill in the ReStore Dartmouth pickup form or email donate@habitatns.ca with a description, photos and your contact information. They also remove full kitchens: "we will remove them free of charge and give you a tax receipt." No geographic boundary for pickups is published, so confirm on the donation line.
  • Mission Thrift Store Halifax — The chain's donate page says pickup is "Not available for every location, please check if you can get a pick-up near you." Their own store finder tags the Halifax store with "Offers Pickup" — but no fee, service area or booking method is published for this store, so call (902) 461-2364 to arrange it.
  • NS Resettlement Society — Nova Horizons — Their page states: "Volunteers are available to pick-up donations and deliver items to their new homes." No fee, service area or booking timeline is published for donation pickups, so use the "Arrange to donate household items" link and expect to confirm details with them.
  • Parker Street Food and Furniture Bank — They do collect: their donation form asks for a "Furniture Pick-Up Location" street address and states that items are "subject to final inspection by drivers." You must upload clear photos of every item or the form will not submit. Their site does not say whether the pickup is free or whether there is a service-area limit — ask when you call 902-425-2125.

Before you load the car

  • Phone first if it is bulky. Capacity changes week to week, and a sofa that cannot be accepted is a wasted trip for you and a wasted hour for them.
  • Clean it. Most refusals are about condition, not category. Textiles must be dry and odour-free or they get thrown out at the back door.
  • Take the parts. Bed frames need their hardware, and a dresser without its drawers is scrap to almost everyone.
  • Do not leave it outside after hours. Anything left at a closed door usually gets rained on, and the charity pays to dispose of it.
  • Ask about the receipt before you hand it over, not after.

Not sure it should be donated at all?

Some of what people give away is worth real money, and some of it will cost a charity money to dispose of. Photograph it and we will tell you which — including when the honest answer is that it is worth nothing and a thrift store is exactly the right place for it. We earn nothing when you donate.

Common questions

Will any of them pick it up from my house in Halifax?
4 of the 11 organizations on this page collect from your home: Habitat for Humanity Nova Scotia ReStore, Mission Thrift Store Halifax, NS Resettlement Society — Nova Horizons, Parker Street Food and Furniture Bank. Collection usually depends on what the item is, what condition it is in, and whether you are inside their catchment. Phone first.
Do I get a tax receipt for donated goods?
Registered charities can issue a receipt for the fair market value of a gift in kind, but many thrift operations do not issue receipts for ordinary household donations because valuing them individually is not practical. Ask before you drop off if the receipt matters to you, and do not assume.
What will nobody take?
Stained or torn upholstery, mattresses in most of the country, broken particleboard furniture, exercise equipment, cribs and car seats past their expiry, and anything that has been in a fire or a flood. Most organizations list their refusals on their own site, and the list above repeats them where they do.
Is my stuff worth money instead of donating it?
Sometimes, and it is worth thirty seconds to find out. A dead car, a garage of tools or mid-century teak can be worth real money. A sofa is usually worth nothing, no matter how good it looks. We will tell you which one you have got before you decide.
