Where to sell clothes and textiles in Winnipeg

6 businesses in and around Winnipeg that buy used clothes from the public — what each takes, and whether you are offered a purchase, a consignment or a loan.

Verified 2026-09-24 from each organization's own website. We are not affiliated with anyone listed here, nobody pays to be included, and we receive nothing if you sell to them.

How to tell what you have got

Before you call anyone, two minutes with the object settles most of it. None of this needs an expert — it needs a flashlight and a look at the back.

  • Teen and young-adult mall brands from the last 12 to 18 monthsIt buys all seasons year-round, takes walk-ins with no appointment, and requires items freshly laundered and free of odours. Sizes are adult only and the ranges are set per store: the London page gives girls 0 to 30 and up to 6X and guys XS to 4X and 28 to 44 inch waist, while the chain's national sell-to-us page gives girls 0 to 6X and guys 28 to 3X. Check your own store's page rather than assuming. No kids or youth sizes anywhere.
  • Premium outerwear and technical kit in near-new conditionBring it in June, not in October. VSP publishes its calendar: "Spring-Summer intake begins in December and Fall-Winter intake begins in June." By the time the weather turns, a shop is already full of parkas and will decline yours whatever the label.
  • Unworn items with the original tags still onKeep them separate and still tagged. Do not launder them or remove the tags to make them look tidy.

Rarely worth anything

Not a rule, and not a reason to bin something without asking. These are the things people most often expect to be worth money and find are not.

  • Mid-market and fast-fashion labels, at a luxury consignor
  • Clothing with stains, balling, repairs or odours
  • Household linens in bulk: towels, sheets, duvet covers, curtains
  • Single socks, single shoes, rags and fabric scraps
  • Underwear and worn-out basics
  • The whole-house pile, taken as a pile
Second-hand textiles are exempt from federal fibre-content labelling if labelled as second-hand Federal (Textile Labelling and Advertising Regulations, C.R.C., c. 1551, s. 9, under the Textile Labelling Act)

Section 9: "A prescribed consumer textile article that is clearly identified by means of a label as a second-hand article is exempt from the application of section 3 of the Act." The regulations do not define "second-hand", and the exemption is conditional on the article carrying that label. Relevant if you are reselling a quantity of clothing as a business rather than giving it away.

Broadway Pawn & Sales

Buys or lends Bring it to them
BuysGold, silver and diamonds, Watches, vintage to new, Jewellery
The dealThe site states plainly: 'We Buy | We Pawn | We Sell', and separately 'We buy and sell gold!!!'. Buying outright is a straight sale - money for the item, item gone. Pawning is a loan against the item, which you must repay to get it back. The categories explicitly named as bought are gold, silver, diamonds and watches. The site also describes itself as 'a music store, a jewelery store, a furniture store, a tool store, a game and movie store and more', so it is worth asking about instruments, tools, furniture and games - but that line describes what they stock, not a published buying list. No loan terms, rates or redemption period are published. Appraisal is in-store only: the site states 'all customers must come to our shop to get the appraisal'. They also state 'We have cheque cashing!!!', which is a separate financial service.
Where612 Broadway, Winnipeg, MB R3C 0W8
Be clear which transaction you are entering. A pawn loan is secured borrowing and the item is forfeited if it is not redeemed; a sale is final. Manitoba's Personal Property Security Act excludes pledgor-and-pawnbroker transactions from the Part 6 default protections that apply to other secured credit. Neither the interest rate nor the redemption period is published on the site, so get both in writing before you sign. Because appraisal is in-store only, budget for the trip, and take photo ID.

Canada Gold - Winnipeg

Buys outright Bring it to them
BuysGold, silver and platinum jewellery, Canadian gold, silver, platinum and palladium bullion, International bullion, Dental gold and other precious metal items, Scrap gold, silver and platinum in any form
The dealOutright purchase. Their sell-to-us page states that specialists will 'evaluate your items in front of you and provide a free, no-obligation offer based on current market prices', that 'No appointments required', and that 'Almost all payouts are completed the same day'. The evaluation happening in front of you is the useful part - you can ask for the weight and the purity reading as they take them. Precious metal is bought on weight and purity, so an item whose value lies in its craftsmanship, maker, brand or age is being valued on the wrong axis here; have anything of that kind assessed by a jeweller or collector first, because a scrap sale is irreversible. Getting a second quote costs only time, and the evaluation is free and carries no obligation.

Chochy's Pawn & Swap Shop

Pawning is a loan secured on the item: you keep the right to redeem it by repaying the loan plus charges within the agreed period, and if you do not redeem it the item is forfeited to the shop. Manitoba's Personal Property Security Act expressly excludes a pledgor-and-pawnbroker transaction from Part 6, the part of the Act that sets out a borrower's rights and remedies when a secured creditor enforces against collateral. This shop publishes no loan terms, no interest rate, no fees and no redemption period, so ask for all of it in writing at the counter before agreeing to anything, and make sure the paperwork says plainly whether you are selling or pawning.

CONFIRMED: Manitoba has no province-wide pawnbroker or secondhand-dealer statute. The index of C.C.S.M. Acts contains no Act titled "Pawnbrokers", "Second-hand", "Secondhand" or "Salvage Dealers". The only provincial mention of a pawnbroker is a definition inside The Personal Property Security Act, C.C.S.M. c. P35, which defines "pawnbroker" as "a person who engages in the business of granting credit to individuals for personal, family or household purposes and who (a) takes and perfects security interests in consumer goods by taking possession of them, or (b) purchases consumer goods under agreements or undertakings, express or implied, that the goods may be repurchased by the seller". ONE PROVISION MATTERS TO ANYONE PAWNING AN ITEM. Section 55(1) of that Act states that Part 6 - "Rights and Remedies on Default" - "does not apply to ... (b) a transaction between a pledgor and a pawnbroker." Part 6 is the part of the Act that gives an ordinary borrower protections when a secured creditor seizes and sells collateral. Those protections do not apply to a pawn transaction. A full text search of the Act returns the word "pawnbroker" only three times: the English definition, the French definition, and this exclusion. The Act sets no licensing, seller-ID, record-keeping, police-reporting, holding-period or redemption requirements for pawnbrokers. NOT CONFIRMED, AND THEREFORE NOT STATED AS FACT: Winnipeg regulates used-goods and precious-metals dealers municipally, but I could not retrieve the text of the City's business licensing by-law or its licensing pages - every City URL I tried returned 404 or was blocked. So this guide does not assert how many activities the City licenses, the definitions or exclusions for "Used Goods Dealer", "Precious Metals Dealer" or "Used Material Yard", whether a Certificate of Record Review Board from the Winnipeg Police Service is required, any holding period before resale, or any police-reporting duty. I also could not verify the Winnipeg Police Service "Operation Identification" program or its phone number. Anyone who needs those specifics should confirm them with City of Winnipeg Licensing and Bylaw Enforcement via 311 before relying on them. SELLER ID, CONFIRMED FROM THE BUYERS THEMSELVES RATHER THAN FROM ANY REGULATOR: Canada Gold's Winnipeg page states you must be "18 years of age or older" and provide "A valid government-issued photo ID". Winnipeg Gold Buyer's site states "we require a piece of photo ID (to help the authorities ensure the items are not stolen)". Both are the businesses' own published policies. Take government-issued photo ID to any of these outlets.
